# Arabic Alphabet Fingerspelling Guide

## What is Fingerspelling?

Fingerspelling is a method of representing letters of the alphabet using hand signs. In the context of Tunisian Sign Language, fingerspelling is used to spell out words letter by letter when there is no established sign for a particular word. Each letter of the Arabic alphabet is assigned a distinct hand shape and position.

**How to perform fingerspelling:**
- Perform each letter sign one at a time.
- Pause briefly between letters so the receiver can distinguish each one.
- Use your dominant hand unless otherwise specified.
- Keep your hand at a consistent position (typically chest to shoulder height).

## Important Notes

### Ha (ح) vs Ha2 (ه)

Arabic has two distinct "h" sounds, and they must be represented with separate signs:

- **ha (ح)** — Index 5, Latin `h`: The "deep H" (حاء). This is a voiceless pharyngeal fricative. It is produced deeper in the throat. In fingerspelling, it uses a distinct hand shape from ha2.
- **ha2 (ه)** — Index 25, Latin `h'`: The "light H" (هاء). This is a voiceless glottal fricative, similar to the English "h". It is produced higher in the throat.

The Latin transliteration uses `h` for ح and `h'` for ه to distinguish them in written form.

### Emphatic Letters (Matjiwra)

Arabic has four "emphatic" or "heavy" consonants. These are produced with the tongue raised toward the roof of the mouth, giving them a darker, more resonant quality:

- **sad (ص)** — Index 13, Latin `s'`: Emphatic S (صاد). A heavy, emphatic version of sin (س).
- **dad (ض)** — Index 14, Latin `d'`: Emphatic D (ضاد). A heavy, emphatic version of dal (د). Arabic is sometimes called "the language of the ض" due to its uniqueness.
- **ta_h (ط)** — Index 15, Latin `t'`: Emphatic T (طاء). A heavy, emphatic version of ta (ت).
- **za (ظ)** — Index 16, Latin `z'`: Emphatic Z (ظاء). A heavy, emphatic version of zay (ز).

In the Latin transliteration, the apostrophe (') distinguishes emphatic letters from their non-emphatic counterparts (e.g., `s` vs `s'`, `d` vs `d'`).

### Control Gestures

Three of the 31 classes are **control gestures**, not letters of the alphabet:

- **space (Index 28)** — Latin `_`: A separator gesture used between words during spelling. Perform this gesture to indicate a word boundary.
- **backspace (Index 29)** — Latin `⌫`: A delete gesture. Perform this to indicate that the previously signed letter should be removed (correcting a mistake).
- **finish (Index 30)** — Latin `⏎`: A finalization gesture. Perform this to indicate that the word or sentence is complete.

These control gestures are essential for practical fingerspelling communication, enabling word separation, error correction, and sentence completion.
